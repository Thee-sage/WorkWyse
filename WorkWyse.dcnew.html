Find out what is actually known about a job.

Job listings go stale, get reposted for months, and sometimes were never really open. WorkWyse collects what people can show — accounts, screenshots, automated checks — and keeps it attached to the claim it supports.

We do not decide whether a job is real. We show you the record and who checked it, and let you judge what it is worth.

What WorkWyse does not claim

That a listing is fake. We have no way to know, and neither does anyone who has not worked there.

That a company is dishonest. A dead application page and six reposts are facts about a listing, not a verdict on an employer.

That our score is objective. It is a summary of the record, it moves when the record moves, and it is always optional to look at.

How this works

A record of what people can show, and who checked it.

WorkWyse is not a verification service and not a review site. It is a public record. Anyone can add to it, everything is attributed, and the gaps are marked as gaps.

What a record is

A record starts when someone pastes a job URL. From that moment two things happen: we begin checking the page automatically, and anyone who has dealt with that listing can file what happened to them.

Everything filed is sorted under the seven questions a job seeker actually asks. A record is not a verdict page — it is those seven questions with whatever answers exist, and the honest blanks where none do.

A claim

Something a person says happened, or something the listing asserts. Published as stated, attributed, never presented as fact.

Evidence

A document or link that supports or contradicts a claim. Stays attached to the claim it concerns, with its check status visible.

An automated check

Machine output about the page itself — whether it responds, whether the text appears elsewhere, when it changed. Never a judgement.

Consensus

What contributors voted, shown raw with its sample size. The cheapest signal here, and weighted accordingly.

A moderator decision

A judgement about a document, never about a job. Published with its reasoning and appealable by anyone it affects.

An employer response

Published unedited, above the reports it answers. Employers are invited whenever evidence is accepted against a listing of theirs.

What the states mean

Each of the seven questions carries one of three states. They describe how much is on record — not how good or bad the job is.

Answered
Answered from evidence

At least one checked document, or an automated check that settles it. You still need to decide what the answer means for you — but the answer itself is not in dispute.

PARTLY
Partly answered

Something is known and something is missing. Usually one account without corroboration, or a fact that answers half the question — “it was real in May” does not tell you about today.

OPEN
Nothing on record

Nobody has answered it. We do not estimate, infer, or fill it with a similar record's data. A page with three of these is telling you something real about how little is known.

And the number?

There is one, and it is deliberately hard to find. It is a weighted summary of the seven answers, it moves several points when one person contributes to a thin record, and it is never shown as a headline. If a score is doing more work in your decision than the accounts on the page, we have designed the page badly.

How moderation works

Moderators check documents. That is the whole job. They confirm a file is what it claims to be, they redact people who did not choose to be here, and they publish their reasoning. They are explicitly forbidden from stating whether a listing is genuine.

THEY DO Verify sender domains and archive timestamps · redact third parties · withhold files that endanger someone · rule on disputes about evidence · publish every decision with a note
They do not Decide whether a job is real · rank employers · remove accounts for being unflattering · edit anyone's words · delete a record because a company asked
If they get it wrong Anyone affected can appeal, including the employer. Appeals are heard by a different moderator, and both the original decision and the outcome stay in the public activity log. Nineteen decisions were overturned last quarter, and all nineteen are still visible.
Why the moderation workspace looks like this site

Moderators see the same record you do, with the same states and the same wording. Nothing is hidden behind an internal view with different language — the only difference is the queue and the decision controls.

What we do not know

This is the most important page on the site, and it is the one we get asked to soften most often.

We cannot tell you a listing is fake.

A dead page, six reposts, and two people who heard nothing are facts. They are consistent with a ghost job and equally consistent with a hiring freeze nobody cleaned up after. We publish the facts and refuse the inference.

We cannot tell you an employer is dishonest.

Most companies never engage with us, and silence is not an admission. A company profile here describes our record, which is a description of who chose to file — not of the organisation.

Our sample is not representative.

People who are hired rarely come back to say so. People who are ghosted sometimes do. Every count on this platform tilts that way, which is why we show sample sizes everywhere and label thin records as thin.

Verified does not mean true.

It means a document came from where it says it came from. A real email can still contain a misleading statement, and we have no way to know which.

If you want a platform that tells you which jobs are fake, we are not it, and we would be lying if we tried. What we can do is make sure that by the time you decide, you know what everyone else has found.
